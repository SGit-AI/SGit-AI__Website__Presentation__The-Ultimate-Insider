Studio experiment E08 · the explainer

The lethal trifecta, frame by frame

The one sentence: an agent with private data, exposure to untrusted content and a way to send outward can be turned by a document into sending that data out; take away any one of the three and that path closes.

The frames (v1)

Seconds from the start; it ends at 48 s and holds still. The line under the drawing changes with each stage.

The source

  • The idea: Simon Willison, The lethal trifecta for AI agents, already the talk's source and listed on the sources page. The slide uses only its three ingredients, as the studio's programme names them: private data, exposure to untrusted content, a way to communicate outward. On the slide they are shortened to "private data", "untrusted content" and "a way out".
  • Words the slide adds: "a document from anyone", "your files", "the agent", "turned", "the outside", "open", "closed", "turn off: closed", and the line under each stage. Each describes the drawing, not a new claim.
  • No numbers. The request asked for none, and the picture needs none: it shows a condition, not a measurement.

Technique

  • One slide on the engine, driven: the SVG is built once, then every frame is drawn from the slide's clock by SLIDE_RENDER, so the player can seek, pause and replay it, and ?static shows the end state. The stages are declared in window.RUV_STEPS: 0, 7, 16, 24, 32 and 41 s.
  • One path, drawn as four steps along one progress value: the document's words reach the agent, the agent is turned, it takes the data, it sends it out. Turning a switch off caps that progress at the wall by that switch, so every "off" shows exactly how far the path gets.
  • E04 v2's language: a path draws itself and changes colour where it crosses the agent; E10's wall is the stop. Three colours of meaning: the agent's red for the agent and what it sends out, purple for the untrusted words, the accent for a switch that is off and its wall. Everything else is neutral. Every text is 20 px or larger on the 1280 by 720 canvas; both themes (?theme=light). It reads no data file and needs no network.

What was left out

  • A real case. EchoLeak, in the studio's injection-sources data, had all three present (email as untrusted content, internal data, a path out) and is labelled there a vendor-confirmed vulnerability. It was left out to keep the slide to one idea, with less text and no numbers; E03 already shows it.
  • Quotations from the source. Only the three ingredients are used; the rest of Willison's argument is for the talk to tell.
  • Other harms. The picture closes one path, the one the trifecta describes. Turning a switch off does not make the agent safe in every other way, and the end line says "that path", not "safe".
  • An "on" and "off" label beside each switch. Tried; it sat on the line. The switch's knob and colour carry the state, and "closed" at the outside says the result.
