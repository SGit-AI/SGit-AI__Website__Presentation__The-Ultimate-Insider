Where every word comes from

  • Only harness.json, the researcher's capture of sharness.ai, read by the slide when it loads, so a corrected file corrects the slide. The probe count and the held count are counted from the file; the environment count too.
  • The researcher's cautions, kept: six of six held on 25 October 2026 (not "24 to 29 October", which is the front page's footnote for a strip that also carries a later figure); five environments, not five platforms, on two kernels, all uid 0; "held" means each probe got the verdict the policy says, one of them an allow.
  • Own site. sharness.ai is the editor's project, and every row on its machines page is its own; no third-party report exists yet. The slide says so.
  • Not contained today. Every result is a policy applied by a session to its own child: a boundary for the child, a setting for the session. A process cannot bind its own session; only the launcher above the agent makes the policy a boundary for the agent, and that launcher is designed, with its first slice (sharness-ai shell) running since 28 October. The slide ends on that caveat.
  • Short words. The tiles name each probe in short words read from the capture (for example "Take 400 MiB under a 128 MiB cap" for bytearray(400 << 20) under max_memory_mb: 128); the table above holds the capture's own. The four steps are the order the request gives, worded from the probes (deny-all; the expected hash; Landlock and a memory cap).
  • The opening row is E09's: its label is the capabilities file's, as E09 and E02 show it; no number from E09 is repeated beyond the 12 of 12 its last line carries.

Technique

  • One slide on the engine, driven: SVG and tiles built once from the data, every frame drawn from the slide's clock by SLIDE_RENDER; ?static shows the end state.
  • The palette, after the editor's review: the agent red only for the agent and its path, the boundary colour (teal) for the policy and what held, the infrastructure colour for the internet and for the allowed path beyond the gate; the piece's ID large at the top right.
  • E04 v2's language for a barrier: here the path stops at the wall, because the wall held.
  • It runs only over HTTP. The slide reads its data with fetch when it loads.
