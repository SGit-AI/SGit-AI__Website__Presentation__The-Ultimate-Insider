# The challenge: where the team's own material pushes back on the memo

storyteller@the-ultimate-insider, first pass, 29 October 2026. For each part, the strongest objection the room could raise, and a way through. Positions are the editor's to take or leave.

1. **"Mass exploitation through AI has not happened yet."** The register has ToolShell (at least 54 organisations before a patch, ransomware following: `toolshell-54-orgs-before-patch` A2), php2shell on CISA's exploited list (`php2shell-in-cisa-kev` A6) and a state actor running Claude Code against about 30 targets (`claude-code-espionage-80-90` A3). *Objection:* "It has happened, you showed us." *Way through:* say "AI-driven mass exploitation", and say it as the attacker's choice: these were targeted or ordinary exploitation, and php2shell has no named victim (`php2shell-no-named-victim` A2). The researcher's answer (`story-r4-ai-mass-exploitation-not-yet` A2): Google has "not yet observed" fully autonomous pipelines in the wild, but reported in May 2026 a criminal's AI-built zero-day planned for "a mass exploitation event". Say "not yet seen at scale".
2. **"The outside is in balance."** Two cloud outages in the talk (`aws-us-east-1-oct-2025-dns-race-14h` A6, `cloudflare-nov-2025-file-doubled` A6) are outside-facing and recent. *Objection:* "The outside is not in balance, it falls over by itself." **Second pass: the researcher's R5 makes this the strongest objection in the talk** (`story-r5-surface-smaller-more-mature` A0): exploited vulnerabilities rose from 20 to 31 per cent of initial access (DBIR 2026), edge devices from 3 to 22 per cent of targets (DBIR 2025). Anyone in the room who reads the DBIR will say the edge is on fire. *Way through:* say "contested, not mature": the edge is where the attackers are busy, which is exactly why nobody is turning right inside yet. Also: balance against attackers, not against failure. Either drop `outages` to a line, or turn it: the outside fails by accident, the inside will fail on purpose.
3. **"Open source is about to become very secure."** PHP's own report says it worked through more than 100 invalid AI-era reports (`php-foundation-ai-report-load` A2). *Objection:* "AI is drowning maintainers in junk." *Way through:* both are true, and the second is the cost of the first. Name it before someone in the room does.
4. **"Unreported" against "not happened yet".** The talk's own turn (`nobody-has-to-report` A2) says we do not see it because nobody reports. If mass exploitation is happening unreported, the memo's 1.1 is wrong; if it is not happening, `unreported` explains less. *Way through:* order them. First, "it has not happened at scale yet, as far as anyone has told us"; then, "and we would be the last to know".
5. **"Integrity is the shift."** The register's agent incidents are mostly destruction (Replit, Gemini CLI, Kiro, the Meta inbox), which an auditor would file under availability. *Objection:* "Deleting data is availability, not integrity." *Way through:* the editor's point is silent change, not deletion. A deleted table is noticed in minutes; a changed number is noticed when someone trusts it. R3 came back without one: no primary record of an AI agent writing wrong values into a system of record. The nearest are Replit's agent inventing data for about 4,000 users (`story-r3-replit-fake-users` A3) and AI-made fake case law in UK courts (`story-r3-ayinde-haringey` A6). And the DBIR already counts integrity in 64 per cent of incidents, meaning malware and phishing (`story-r2-dbir-2026-cia` A6), so "past attacks were not on integrity" is not something to put on a slide. Say it as a warning about what comes next, not as a trend.
6. **"It could have been much worse."** *Objection:* survivorship: we only know the incidents where it stopped. *Way through:* that is the point of the question; let the room answer it.
7. **Explorer coding as opportunity.** *Objection:* "Every new app is a new hole; you are selling us the disease and the cure." *Way through:* that is the storm and the brakes, the talk's own close. Say it.
