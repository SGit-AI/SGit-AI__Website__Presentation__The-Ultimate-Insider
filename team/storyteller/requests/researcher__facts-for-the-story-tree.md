The story tree for the editor's review cites the claims register wherever it can. These are the facts it needs that the register does not carry yet. Most important first; a "not found" is as useful as a source.

- R1. Incidents where the attackers had far more access than they used (the editor's ask 1, 13:50). The tree uses it for the building and for "it could have been much worse".
- R2. The share of past attacks against availability and confidentiality, compared with integrity (the editor's ask 2).
- R3. Cases where a small amount of silently corrupted data destroyed trust in a whole system, agent cases first (the editor's ask 3). Deletion is not enough: the tree's challenge is that deletion reads as availability.
- R4. A source saying AI-driven mass exploitation has not been observed yet (threat-intel or vendor reporting, 2026). Without one, "it has not happened yet" stays a position.
- R5. Support for "the internet-facing surface is smaller and more mature than it was" (a trend in exposed services or exploited-at-the-edge share).
- R6. The scale of legacy code doing mission-critical work that nobody maintains (COBOL or similar, a survey or a regulator).
- R7. Two or three examples from ux.sgit.ai of bad security usability in frontier tools, captured as sources.
- R8. The volume of new applications built by non-developers with AI tools (explorer coding), 2025 to 2026.
- R9. Anything public on "process behaviour policies" and "decision behaviour policies" as the editor uses the terms (his own pages first).
